# 1-D ChDR presentation

This TeXShop-ready Beamer presentation uses a common beam (60 MeV kinetic
energy, 1 nC, and 1 mm rms longitudinal length) for two applications:

1. Full-bunch length diagnostics: infer the Gaussian envelope length from the
   coherent spectral roll-off, starting with a simpler GHz validation case.
2. Fine structure and microbunching: study optical-scale bunching near 0.5 um
   against the unmodulated fixed-`N` shot-noise reference.

The formula slides define the variables, distinguish energy flux from induced
work, and explain the statistical intensity terms. References include precise
equation numbers for the fixed-count moments. An appendix derives the SI
Fourier normalization used by this implementation.

The measurement comparison adds a figure of published GHz detector/filter
bands, a short optical comparison and six experimental references. The fuller
discussion is in Section 5 of
`../../ideas-lit-reseach/chdr_literature_review.tex`. Current 1 nC / 1 mm
parameters are explicitly distinguished from that report's earlier baseline.

Regenerate the figures and PDF from this directory:

```sh
make figures
make pdf
```

The model figures are generated from `../chdr_1d.py`. The rms duration is derived
from the exact 1 mm rms length and electron velocity (about 3.33576 ps).
The optical spectrum uses a 10 micrometre gap and real constant
`epsilon_r=2.13`; the gap scan varies this distance. The density diagnostic
uses a separate uniform 1 mm-cubed volume, not the volume of the Gaussian beam.
The material is illustrative and the current optical plot is a smooth-bunch
noise reference, not a computed microbunched signal. The intrinsic bunching
noise level is not a detector sensitivity estimate.

The comparison figure is generated by
`../../ideas-lit-reseach/compare_measurement_bands.py`. `make figures` also
regenerates this plot and copies its wide slide version into `figures/`.
`make literature-figure` updates only the literature comparison.

Open `chdr_1d_presentation.tex` in TeXShop or use the Makefile to compile it.
References are written directly in the source; BibTeX is not required.
